The reef visualizes the prior

A VAE compares each encoded distribution with a shared reference distribution called the prior. Here, the prior is a standard normal distribution centered at the reef.

The prior is the reference shape that the model is encouraged to organize its latent distributions toward.

The distance d measures how far the encoded mean is from the prior mean. It does not yet include spread mismatch, so it is not the complete KL divergence.

The identity matrix I means the displayed prior has unit variance in both latent dimensions and no correlation between them.

In this tutorial, the reef radius equals one latent unit, matching one standard-deviation unit of the prior along any direction. It is a reference contour, not a boundary; the prior continues smoothly beyond it.

The rings in the artwork are qualitative density guides, not exact probability boundaries.

The tutorial lets you move designed distributions manually. A neural-network encoder is not repositioning them.

Next, KL divergence will combine location and spread into one measure of prior mismatch.

KL divergence measures that mismatch

An encoded distribution can differ from the prior in two ways: its mean can be in a different location, and its spread can have a different size.

KL divergence = location mismatch + spread mismatch

The logarithm is the natural logarithm, so the result is expressed in nats.

Each value above is rounded to two decimal places independently, so the two components may not appear to add up exactly to the total. The total is calculated at full precision.

This is DKL(qφ(z|x) ‖ p(z)). KL divergence has a direction and is not a symmetric geometric distance.

At the reef center, location mismatch is zero. KL divergence can still remain above zero when σ is not 1.

KL divergence reaches zero only when μ = (0,0) and σ = 1.
